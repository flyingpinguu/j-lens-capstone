Inside the LLM

Reading a model's mind before it speaks

We watch a language model's internal state as a prompt-injection attack hits it — and predict whether it's about to leak a secret, before it writes a single word.

The idea · for the room

What we built, in four points

  1. The risk
    An assistant is given a secret and told never to reveal it. Prompt injections — "ignore your rules", "let's role-play", "fill in the blank" — try to pull it out.
  2. The lens
    Instead of only judging the answer, we look inside. At each of the model's 32 layers, the Jacobian Lens reads its internal state and shows the ranked list of words it is closest to saying next — its half-formed answer, layer by layer, before it commits.
  3. The tell
    We track one thing: how high the secret token climbs in that internal ranking, before the model answers. In the diagram it sits far down at rank 144 — an attack that is about to succeed pushes it toward the top.
  4. The prediction
    Four features from that pre-response state feed a classifier that flags a likely leak — ROC-AUC ≈ 0.73, validated across held-out attack categories.

The evidence · aggregate signal

The signal lives in the last few layers

A leak isn't decided early. Per-layer accuracy stays near — or below — chance through most of the network, then climbs sharply in the final layers, peaking at layer 27 (AUC 0.77). The model's commitment to reveal the secret forms late, in the last handful of layers, moments before it speaks — which is exactly the window a guardrail needs.
